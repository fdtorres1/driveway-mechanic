It's January, it's 20 degrees outside, and your heater is blowing cold air. Few things are more miserable. The good news? Your car's heating system is actually pretty simple, and most problems come down to a handful of things you can check in your driveway without any special tools.

## How Your Car's Heater Works

Your car's heater is basically a tiny radiator behind the dashboard called the heater core. Hot engine coolant flows through it, and the blower fan pushes cabin air across it — that's where your warm air comes from. A blend door controls how much air passes through the heater core versus bypassing it, which is how your temperature dial works.

So for heat to work, you need: enough coolant, hot coolant (engine at operating temperature), flow through the heater core, and the blend door in the right position. When any of those fail, you get cold air.

## Prevention Tips

Your car's heating system is reliable, but a little attention goes a long way:

- **Check your coolant level monthly**, especially in fall before the cold hits. It takes 30 seconds and prevents the most common heater failure.
- **Change coolant on schedule** — every 30,000-50,000 miles for conventional coolant, or per your owner's manual. Old coolant loses its corrosion protection and can clog the heater core with scale and debris.
- **Don't mix coolant types.** Green, orange, pink, and blue coolants use different chemistry. Mixing them can create gel-like sludge that clogs your heater core and radiator. When in doubt, use a universal coolant like Prestone.
- **Fix small coolant leaks early.** A slow drip from a hose clamp is a $0 fix with a screwdriver. Ignoring it until the system runs low can leave you without heat — or worse, can overheat the engine.
- **If your temp gauge runs cold consistently,** replace the thermostat. It's a $10-$20 part and prevents poor fuel economy, increased emissions, and obviously, no heat.

A car heater blowing cold is usually one of the simplest problems to diagnose. Check your coolant level, feel both heater hoses, and listen for the blend door actuator. Nine times out of ten, you'll find the answer in under 15 minutes — and the fix is often just a jug of coolant.
