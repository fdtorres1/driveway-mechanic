You're sitting at a red light and the engine just… quits. Or it stumbles and nearly dies when you shift into drive. Idle stalling is one of the most common driveability complaints, and while it feels alarming, the cause is usually something simple and fixable.

Your engine needs three things to idle smoothly: the right amount of air, the right amount of fuel, and a reliable spark. When idle stalling happens, one of those three is off. Let's figure out which one.

## Start With the Codes

Before touching anything, plug in an OBD-II scanner. Idle stalling often sets codes that point you right at the problem:

- **P0505-P0507** — Idle Air Control (IAC) system malfunction
- **P0171/P0174** — System too lean (vacuum leak, weak fuel pump, or dirty MAF)
- **P0300** — Random misfire (could be spark, fuel, or compression)
- **P0101** — MAF sensor range/performance issue

Even if no codes are stored, scan anyway. Some issues only set "pending" codes that won't turn on the check engine light.

<Callout tone="tip">
**No scanner? No problem.** Most auto parts stores (AutoZone, O'Reilly, Advance) will scan your codes for free. Write them down and come back home to fix the issue.
</Callout>

<Callout tone="note">
**About the idle relearn:** After cleaning the throttle body, many cars need to "relearn" their idle. Symptoms include rough idle for a few minutes, slight stalling on the first startup, or a higher-than-normal idle that settles down. Most cars relearn automatically after 10-15 minutes of driving. Some (especially Nissan and Subaru) have a specific procedure — search your year/make/model + "idle relearn" if your car is fussy after cleaning.
</Callout>

## Prevention Tips

Stalling issues love neglected engines. A few easy habits go a long way:

- **Replace your air filter on schedule** (every 15,000-30,000 miles). A dirty air filter makes everything downstream dirtier — the MAF, the throttle body, everything.
- **Use quality fuel.** Cheap gas with lots of ethanol can leave deposits on intake valves and injectors. An occasional tank of Top Tier fuel helps.
- **Don't ignore a rough idle.** If your car idles a little rough but doesn't stall yet, it's heading toward stalling. Fix it while it's easy.
- **Check vacuum hoses during oil changes.** While you're under the hood, give every rubber hose a squeeze. Cracks, soft spots, or disconnected ends are easy to catch visually.
- **Clean the throttle body every 30,000-50,000 miles** as preventive maintenance. It takes 15 minutes and a $6 can of cleaner. Think of it like flossing for your engine.

Idle stalling is usually a dirty throttle body or a small vacuum leak. Grab a can of cleaner, spend 20 minutes under the hood, and there's a great chance you'll fix it yourself.
