If you own a 2015–2023 Ford F-150 with the 3.5L EcoBoost, you're driving one of the most popular trucks on the road — and one of the easiest to maintain yourself. An oil change is the single most important thing you can do to keep that twin-turbo engine running strong, and the good news is that Ford made it surprisingly straightforward with a top-mounted cartridge oil filter.

No crawling under the truck to wrestle with a spin-on filter. No burned knuckles on exhaust pipes. Just a simple drain-and-fill that'll save you $40–$65 every time compared to taking it to the shop.

Let's walk through it.

## Step-by-Step: Changing the Oil

### 1. Warm Up and Elevate

Start the engine and let it idle for 2–3 minutes. Warm oil flows better and carries more contaminants out. Then shut it off, engage the parking brake, and drive the truck onto ramps (or jack it up and place jack stands under the frame rails).

### 2. Open the Hood and Remove the Oil Fill Cap

Pop the hood and remove the oil fill cap on the passenger-side valve cover. This breaks the vacuum and helps the oil drain faster.

### 3. Remove the Oil Filter Housing Cap

Locate the oil filter housing on the driver's side top of the engine. Use the 76mm cap wrench or insert a 3/8 in. ratchet directly into the cap's built-in square drive. Turn counterclockwise to remove. Pull the old cartridge filter out with the cap — it clips onto the cap's center post.

### 4. Drain the Oil

Slide under the truck and locate the drain plug on the bottom of the oil pan (driver's side, rear of the pan). Place your drain pan underneath. Remove the plug with a 15mm socket — be ready, the oil comes out fast. Let it drain for at least 5 minutes.

### 5. Install the New Filter Cartridge

Pull the old cartridge off the cap's center post and press the new Motorcraft FL-500S filter onto it. Check that the O-ring on the housing cap is in good shape (a new one usually comes with the filter). Lightly coat the O-ring with fresh oil. Reinstall the housing cap and torque to **18 ft-lbs**.

### 6. Reinstall the Drain Plug

Once the oil has fully drained, wipe the drain plug area clean. If your drain plug gasket looks crushed or worn, replace it. Thread the plug in by hand first (to avoid cross-threading), then torque to **25 ft-lbs**. No more.

### 7. Fill with Fresh Oil

Using a funnel, pour in 6 quarts of 5W-30 full synthetic oil through the fill cap opening. Replace the oil fill cap.

### 8. Check Your Work

Start the engine and let it idle for 30 seconds. Check under the truck for any drips at the drain plug. Check around the filter housing cap for leaks. Shut the engine off, wait 2 minutes, then check the dipstick. The level should be right at or just below the full mark.

### 9. Reset the Oil Life Monitor

Turn the ignition to the ON position (don't start the engine). Press the gas pedal and brake pedal simultaneously and hold for about 3 seconds. The "Oil Change Required" message will flash and then reset. Release the pedals. On 2018+ models, you can also reset via the instrument cluster menu: Settings → Vehicle → Oil Life Reset.

<Callout tone="note">
  **Don't forget to reset the oil life monitor.** If you skip this step, you'll get a premature oil change reminder that doesn't reflect your fresh oil. It won't hurt anything, but it's annoying.
</Callout>

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Overtightening the drain plug.** The F-150 has an aluminum oil pan. Strip those threads and you're looking at a $300+ repair or a helicoil insert. Stick to 25 ft-lbs.
- **Using conventional oil.** The 3.5L EcoBoost runs hot with twin turbos. It requires full synthetic oil. Conventional oil breaks down too fast under those conditions.
- **Forgetting the filter housing O-ring.** If you reuse a dried-out or cracked O-ring, the housing will weep oil. The new filter usually includes a fresh O-ring — use it.
- **Overfilling.** Six quarts is the correct amount with a filter change. Adding extra "for good measure" can cause foaming, oil aeration, and even blow out seals. Check the dipstick.
- **Not warming the engine first.** Cold oil is thick and clings to internal surfaces. A quick 2–3 minute warm-up lets you drain significantly more old oil out.

## Maintenance Tips

- **Follow the oil life monitor**, not a fixed mileage interval. Ford's system accounts for driving conditions. Most owners see a change interval between 7,500 and 10,000 miles.
- **Keep a record** of every oil change — mileage, date, oil brand, and filter. This is invaluable for warranty claims and resale value.
- **Inspect the drain plug gasket** every change and replace it every other change. A $3 gasket prevents a messy, slow leak.
- **Buy oil and filters in bulk** when they go on sale. Motorcraft oil frequently drops to around $25 for a 6-pack during Amazon sales events.

<Callout tone="tip">
  **First time doing this?** Budget an extra 15–20 minutes. By your second or third oil change, you'll have this down to a 25-minute job — faster than sitting in the waiting room at a quick lube shop.
</Callout>

Changing your own oil is the gateway to doing your own maintenance. It's simple, it saves real money over time, and it gives you the satisfaction of knowing exactly what's going into your engine. Your F-150's EcoBoost will thank you with hundreds of thousands of reliable miles.
