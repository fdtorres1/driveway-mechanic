You jump-start your car, drive around for a while, and the next morning it's dead again. Or maybe it dies after sitting for a weekend. Either way, the frustration is real — and replacing the battery doesn't always fix it.

A dying battery is a symptom. Let's find the disease.

## The Three Usual Suspects

When a battery keeps dying, it's almost always one of three things:

1. **The battery itself is bad** — old, worn out, or has a dead cell
2. **Something is draining it while the car is off** — that's a parasitic draw
3. **The alternator isn't charging it properly** — so it never gets fully recharged while driving

We'll test for all three.

## Prevention Tips

Keep your battery happy and it'll keep starting your car:

- **Drive regularly.** Short trips (under 15 minutes) don't fully recharge the battery. If you mostly make short trips, consider a maintenance charger.
- **Check terminals twice a year.** Clean off any corrosion and tighten connections. Takes 5 minutes.
- **Be mindful of aftermarket accessories.** Dashcams, stereos, and phone chargers wired to constant power are the #1 cause of parasitic drain. Wire them to a switched circuit or use a dashcam with a built-in voltage cutoff.
- **Test your battery's age.** Most batteries have a date sticker. If it's past 4 years, keep a close eye on it — or replace it proactively before it leaves you stranded.
- **Use a battery maintainer** if the car sits for more than a week at a time. A trickle charger keeps the battery at full charge without overcharging.

A dead battery is annoying, but it's almost always a simple diagnosis. Grab a multimeter, work through the steps, and you'll have your answer in under an hour. Most of the time, it's either a tired old battery or something silly like a trunk light left on.
